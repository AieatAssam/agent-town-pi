Jam

No crash. No lane closure. One driver taps the brakes, and a slowdown can travel backward through the line like a wave. Tune the spacing and the tap; watch whether traffic recovers or locks up.

Cars follow their neighbors; the orange one taps its brakes once. This is a toy model, not a forecast.

Why does the jam move backward?

Each driver reacts to the gap ahead, but not instantly. A little braking can make the next driver brake harder, then the next. The cars still move forward while the slow patch drifts upstream. In real traffic, small differences in reaction and spacing can turn a harmless correction into stop-and-go waves.
