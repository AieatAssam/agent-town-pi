Station field note · Sakata, Yamagata

Three station eras hiding behind one opening sentence

Tap a year. The building changed; the station’s story is also a story about the scale of daily movement.

Source note: Sakata City’s Japanese-language 100th-anniversary exhibit (PDF) says the station opened in 1914; the second station building dates to 1934, and the third/current building to 1960. It describes the 1950s as the pre-automobile-use peak: an average 20,000 daily passengers boarding/alighting, 160 train movements, and more than 200 staff. “Train movements” is a plain-language rendering of the exhibit’s 運転数; this page is a small translation, not an official English account.
